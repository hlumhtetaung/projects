**Sampling**



what is a population?

* Population is the total size of the dataset or group.

Correction: A population isn't just the size (the number $N$), but the entire complete set or group of all items/people you want to study (e.g., all voters in a country, all transactions in a database).



what is a sample?

* Sample is the selected data or people from the population using various sampling methods. Samples allow scientist to learn about the dataset without needing to use the whole set.



what is a parameter?

* From what I remember, parameter is the numerical characteristic of the population. For example, if a chef were to take the sample spoon from a pot of soup, the number of scoops that the chef can make is parameter.

Soup Analogy Fix: The parameter is the true, actual saltiness (or average flavor) of the entire 50-gallon pot. The number of scoops is just the population size ($N$).



what is a statistic?

Answer: A statistic is a numerical metric calculated directly from sample data (e.g., the sample mean $\\bar{x}$ or sample standard deviation $s$). We use sample statistics to estimate unknown population parameters.Soup Analogy: The saltiness level the chef tastes on their single sample spoon is the statistic.



what is random sampling?

* Random sampling is the sampling method where random people or data are selected from the population.
* Everyone has equal chance of being chosen unless they are in groups.
* There are two sampling - replacing and without replacing.



what is stratified sampling?

* When data are stratified, random sampling can cause bias.
* Stratified sampling is when choosing random number of people from each group.

\* \*\*Correction\*\*: We don't stratify because random sampling causes bias; we stratify to \*\*guarantee fair representation\*\* for small or important sub-groups (\*\*strata\*\*), such as age brackets or geographic regions. You divide the population into homogeneous strata first, and then draw random samples from \*each\* stratum.



what is sampling bias?

* Bias in sampling. When researchers choose those who are close to them or targeting specific groups instead of public opinion.



what is sampling variability?

* Each sample is different from another. For example, if the first scoop contained a salt block from the pot of soup, the first sample will taste much heavier than the rest.



what is sample size?

* Sample size is the size of sample to be taken from the population. For example, In the pot and scoop sample example, the sample size can be teaspoon or a large spoon. Depending on the sample size variability can decrease.



What is standard error?

* From what I remember, standard error is the difference or something between data in sample.

Answer: Standard Error measures how much sample statistics (like the sample mean $\\bar{x}$) bounce around from sample to sample due to sampling variability.Standard Deviation ($s$) = Spread of individual data points in one sample.Standard Error ($SE$) = Spread of sample averages across many repeated samples.Formula: SE = s\\sqrt{n}
